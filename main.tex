\begin{quote}
\textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{quote}

Local health units frequently rely on manual record-keeping protocols
and fragmented data registries, which can impede routine surveillance
and rapid outbreak tracking. To address these operational constraints,
this study develops a local-first dengue surveillance and decision
support system optimized for local health units in Davao City. It is
engineered with a Python-based computational pipeline and a relational
SQLite architecture protected by SQLCipher 256-bit AES encryption at
rest; the platform enforces data security through Werkzeug password
hashing, secure temporary credential provisioning, and Role-Based Access
Control (RBAC) to ensure strict compliance with the Data Privacy Act (RA
10173).

The system\textquotesingle s core integrates a dual-analytic approach:
historical non-parametric 75th percentile (P75) baselines establish
real-time epidemic risk tiers (Green, Yellow, Red), while a Random
Forest Regressor generates 1-week-ahead predictive case trajectories. To
support operational workflows, the platform automates early warning
status notifications and populates an intervention priority watchlist
tracking barangay-level case activity (High Risk, Rising, Stable). By
combining historical statistical thresholds with machine learning
forecasting within a secure, local-first interface, the research
provides a practical digital tool for municipal health monitoring.

\textbf{Keywords:} Public Health Informatics, Dengue Surveillance,
Local-First Architecture, Statistical Epidemic Thresholds, Random Forest
Regressor, Time-Series Forecasting, Data Privacy Act (RA 10173),
Role-Based Access Control

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{1.1 Background of the
Study}}{1.1 Background of the Study}}\label{background-of-the-study}

The management of municipal epidemiological data requires structured
digital approaches that bridge aggregate reporting with operational
clarity. Modern health informatics frameworks emphasize spatial and
temporal disaggregation, wherein surveillance is executed at granular
administrative levels to capture localized transmission dynamics. Within
the Philippine health ecosystem, consolidating community-level data into
standard operational workflows depends on structured digital protocols
(Sylim et al., 2022). In urbanized administrative divisions such as
Davao City, monitoring health metrics across multiple barangays involves
considerable logistical complexity, highlighting the need for localized
tracking mechanisms to observe active Dengue transmission trends.

At the primary care level, municipal health data management frequently
relies on fragmented spreadsheets or manual logs, which can introduce
transcription bottlenecks and data synchronization delays (Kaboré et
al., 2022). Routine reviews of public health monitoring indicate that
traditional passive reporting methods using simple moving averages often
fail to flag early seasonal surges, as historical baselines can be
distorted by data anomalies (Eligado et al., 2024). Furthermore, spatial
analyses demonstrate that vector-borne disease recurrence exhibits
distinct geographic patterns, reinforcing the necessity for localized
time-series evaluations (Olana et al., 2025).

To establish reliable outbreak warnings, public health systems utilize
historical statistical thresholds to identify abnormal case surges.
Non-parametric percentile thresholding models provide robust mechanisms
for establishing baseline activity without being heavily skewed by data
extremes or outlier values (Ouédraogo et al., 2023; Rysava et al.,
2026). When paired with predictive algorithms such as Random Forest
Regression, which effectively capture non-linear temporal trends in
epidemiological data (Zhao et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025) these
systems can support routine early warning evaluations. Integrating such
analytical methods into rule-based decision support logic provides
health personnel with clear operational guidance during high-caseload
periods (Nguyen \& Ming, 2023).

To address these data processing and security constraints, this study
presents a local-first Web-Based Public Health Decision Support System
tailored for Dengue surveillance in Davao City (2023-2026). Developed
using a Python processing pipeline and an encrypted relational SQLite
architecture via SQLCipher (Zhang \& Yin, 2021), the system operates on
a local deployment model to maintain data availability in
low-connectivity environments (Paluyo et al., 2022; Bodducherla, 2026)
while complying with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173). By
structuring data ingestion, descriptive spatial tracking, predictive
regression, and rule-based alert triggers, the system provides a
practical decision-support tool for local health monitoring.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{1.2.1 General
Problem}}{1.2.1 General Problem}}\label{general-problem}

Local health units tracking localized vector-borne diseases like Dengue
frequently face limitations in rapid data aggregation and predictive
analytics. Conventional tracking workflows rely heavily on manual record
entry or rigid spreadsheet templates, delaying the identification of
epidemiological trends. Furthermore, these systems often lack integrated
mechanisms to automatically compute historical epidemic baselines,
forecast near-term case trajectories, and prioritize community-level
interventions under secure multi-user role constraints. To address these
computational and decision-support challenges, this research
investigates the design, implementation, and technical evaluation of
LokalHealth, a local-first, web-based epidemiological surveillance and
decision-support system.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{1.5 Scope and
Limitations}}{1.5 Scope and Limitations}}\label{scope-and-limitations}

The technical scope of this study comprises the development and
evaluation of a Web-Based Public Health Decision Support System
incorporating an integrated analytical framework for localized Dengue
surveillance in Davao City, utilizing case data from 2023 to 2026. The
software architecture consists of a Python analytics processing engine
and a relational SQLite database configured for local-first operations
to support local data query execution during network disconnection
(Paluyo et al., 2022).

The dataset is defined by eight standardized Dengue case variables:
Morbidity Week, Morbidity Month, District, Barangay, Age, Sex, Clinical
Classification, and Case Classification. The system processes these
attributes through three core analytical components: Descriptive
analytics generating temporal distribution curves and barangay
distribution summaries; Predictive analytics calculating 75th percentile
historical baselines and Random Forest regression case trajectories
(Ouédraogo et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2020); and Prescriptive decision
support logic executing rule-based alert triggers (Green for Normal,
Yellow for Warning, and Red for High Risk Level). Data management
complies with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173) by
excluding personally identifiable information, including patient names,
street addresses, and contact details. Access governance is structured
through Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), defining operational
privileges for Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) and City Health
Officers.

System evaluation is limited to software performance metrics,
specifically database query latency (in milliseconds), offline-to-online
data synchronization consistency, and system interface efficiency
evaluated through technical performance profiling and component
response-time verification. The system does not include automated
diagnostic algorithms, clinical decision support for patient treatment,
or biological sensor integration. Offline database capabilities are
limited to local record entry, querying, and reporting, requiring active
network connectivity solely for central administrative database
synchronization. Spatial and temporal analytical functions process
municipal-level records across Davao City, with system deployment and
interface assessments structured around operational workflows typical of
local health units such as those in Talomo District.

\textbf{2.1 Municipal Epidemiological Surveillance and Barangay
Realities}

\textbf{2.1.1 The Philippine Digital Health Landscape and Local
Surveillance Constraints}

Public health information management in the Philippines involves
operational friction between centralized electronic health frameworks
and primary health facility infrastructure. While national strategies
encourage interoperable digital systems (Sylim et al., 2022), data
management at the primary care level frequently depends on manual logs
and retrospective batch reporting. Barangay Health Workers (BHWs) act as
primary data collectors within resource-constrained settings
characterized by variable network availability, hardware constraints,
and administrative workloads (De Mesa et al., 2023). Consequently,
routine surveillance records often encounter transmission delays before
reaching municipal repositories, impacting the timeliness of local data
visibility (Hartigan-Go et al., 2024).

\textbf{2.1.2 Dengue Epidemiology and Granular Surveillance in Davao
City}

In municipal settings such as Davao City, monitoring vector-borne
transmission across 182 barangays involves distinct data collection and
tracking challenges. Urban density, environmental variations, and
population movement patterns contribute to variable Dengue distribution
trends across districts.

While macro-level health statistics provide broad baseline trends,
operational monitoring requires granular, case-level records. Public
health informatics literature indicates that capturing standardized case
attributes (Morbidity Week, Morbidity Month, District, Barangay, Age,
Sex, Clinical Classification, and Case Classification) at the primary
collection point establishes the necessary data baseline required to
execute localized threshold tracking and support operational planning
(Olana et al., 2025; Paluyo et al., 2022).

\textbf{2.2 Technologies and Methodologies for Dengue Health
Informatics}

\textbf{2.2.1 Primary Data Digitization and Schema Normalization}

Transitioning from conventional paper logbooks to structured electronic
registries is an essential prerequisite for municipal epidemiological
surveillance. Replacing manual logs with digital intake pipelines
enables primary health units to eliminate recording delays inherent in
batch reporting. However, digital health initiatives in
resource-constrained municipal environments encounter operational
friction when administrative workflows overburden health personnel
(Ngusie et al., 2021). Software architectures must therefore align
system usability with structural data constraints to sustain user
engagement among primary healthcare workers (Whitehead et al., 2023).

To ensure high-integrity intake without redundancy, relational database
models built on Structured Query Language (SQL) enforce necessary
mathematical schema constraints. Applying formal normalization rules to
epidemiological variables prevents record duplication during
high-throughput ingestion (Alderden et al., 2023). Structuring core
attributes including Morbidity Week, Morbidity Month, District,
Barangay, Age, Sex, Clinical Classification, and Case Classification
within a normalized schema facilitates rapid query performance and
long-term longitudinal evaluation without compromising record validity.

\textbf{2.2.2 Edge Execution, Local-First Storage, and Secure Database
Architecture}

Deploying web-based decision support systems in settings with variable
network access requires shifting away from always-online cloud designs
(Kaboré et al., 2022). Local-first edge computing models resolve these
network limitations by processing core database transactions directly on
client hardware (Paluyo et al., 2022). As shown in edge computing
frameworks requiring stringent security, operating localized databases
with hardware-level encryption preserves continuous system availability
while safeguarding confidential health repositories from unauthorized
exposure (Bodducherla, 2026).

Integrating a local SQLite database engine with Python processing
scripts allows local health facilities to maintain uninterrupted record
entry, local querying, and offline report generation during network
outages (Krishnapur et al., 2026). Furthermore, protecting database
files at rest through standard SQLCipher 256-bit AES encryption ensures
defense against physical hardware compromise (Zhang \& Yin, 2021). Cloud
database synchronization operates as an asynchronous background routine,
preserving local system utility regardless of connectivity state.

\textbf{2.2.3 Multi-Component Analytics and Decision Support
Methodologies}

Modern epidemiological decision support systems utilize multi-tiered
analytical workflows to convert transactional case records into
actionable public health intelligence. To structure the progression from
raw intake data to decision support, the platform implements a
three-tier computational engine:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Descriptive Analytics: Serves as the primary visualization layer,
  aggregating patient attributes into temporal morbidity trend charts,
  categorical distributions (by age, sex, and clinical classification),
  and ranked barangay-level risk distribution tables. This provides
  health officers with immediate temporal visibility and structured
  spatial breakdowns across Davao City without relying on complex GIS
  map rendering (Olana et al., 2025).
\item
  Predictive Modeling (P75 Baseline Limits): Implements non-parametric
  statistical methods, utilizing historical 75th percentile (P75)
  baseline calculations to establish dynamic epidemic thresholds. By
  evaluating historical case distributions, this method accounts for
  seasonal vector dynamics and identifies statistical surge deviations
  prior to peak periods without skewing from extreme data outliers
  (Ouédraogo et al., 2023; Rysava et al., 2026).
\item
  Predictive Forecasting (Random Forest Regression): Incorporates
  tree-based ensemble machine learning models, specifically a Random
  Forest Regressor, to project 1-week-ahead case trajectories.
  Comparative health informatics literature demonstrates that Random
  Forest models capture complex non-linear temporal trends with high
  precision compared to standard linear baselines (Zhao et al., 2020;
  Kuo et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025).
\item
  Decision Support Protocols: Converts threshold metrics and model
  outputs into operational decision support using deterministic
  rule-based logic. By evaluating active caseloads against historical
  limits, the platform automatically generates standardized early
  warning triggers (Green for Normal, Yellow for Warning, and Red for
  High-Risk Alert) paired with targeted intervention protocols to guide
  municipal response efforts (Nguyen \& Ming, 2023).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{2.2.4 Health Data Security, Regulatory Compliance, and
Governance}

Integrating security controls into municipal health architectures is
both a regulatory requirement and a prerequisite for data integrity.
Compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)
requires point-of-entry de-identification, which is achieved by
excluding direct identifiers such as patient names, street addresses,
and contact numbers from the relational schema in favor of
system-generated internal keys. System access governance is maintained
through Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), restricting Barangay Health
Workers to local entry functions while reserving system administration
and account management privileges for City Health Officers or the
System Administrator.

At the application layer, credential protection utilizes salted
key-stretching algorithms via Werkzeug\textquotesingle s native security
utilities (werkzeug.security) to mitigate unauthorized access attempts.
Furthermore, local database files and configuration keys in edge
environments are secured using 256-bit AES encryption via SQLCipher to
protect against local hardware compromise.

These security mechanisms operate within a structured data management
framework, which governs records across key operational phases: data
creation, secure local storage, analytical processing, and longitudinal
retention, ensuring that historical baselines remain intact for
multi-year epidemiological comparison (Oktaviana et al., 2025; Rahul \&
Banyal, 2020).

\textbf{2.2.5 Synthesis, Research Gaps, and Proposed Technical Solution}

Public health informatics literature outlines distinct structural
attributes characterizing contemporary municipal surveillance
environments. First, a spatial resolution variance frequently occurs
because regional monitoring frameworks prioritize macro-level
administrative aggregates, leaving local health units to handle
high-resolution barangay tracking independently (Sylim et al., 2022;
Olana et al., 2025). Second, many standard operational workflows depend
on continuous network connectivity, presenting logistical challenges in
environments subject to intermittent infrastructure availability (Kaboré
et al., 2022). Third, standard health informatics architectures often
separate transactional data storage from active decision support
processing (Ngusie et al., 2021; Ouédraogo et al., 2023).

To address these architectural parameters, this research implements a
Web-Based Decision Support System configured for municipal Dengue
surveillance in Davao City. To manage spatial resolution alignment, raw
multi-year surveillance logs are processed through an automated schema
normalization pipeline that standardizes variable attributes across
reporting periods into core operational parameters (Morbidity Month,
District, Barangay, Age, Sex, Clinical Classification, Case
Classification, and Morbidity Week) for localized temporal and spatial
tracking (Alderden et al., 2023). Network constraints are addressed
through a local-first SQLite architecture paired with Python processing
pipelines, allowing offline data management, local querying, and
analytical generation prior to scheduled database synchronization
(Kleppmann et al., 2019; Paluyo et al., 2022; Krishnapur et al., 2026).
To integrate analytics within local workflows, the platform incorporates
a multi-component analytical framework: descriptive data aggregations
for temporal and categorical visualization, historical 75th percentile
(P75) threshold computations for non-parametric baseline comparisons
(Ouédraogo et al., 2023; Rysava et al., 2026), Random Forest regression
modeling for short-term case trajectory estimation (Zhao et al., 2020;
Kuo et al., 2024), and rule-based decision support logic for operational
guidance (Nguyen \& Ming, 2023).

Furthermore, data protection, access governance, and encryption
mechanisms are integrated to maintain data confidentiality, system
integrity, and compliance with RA 10173 across municipal operations
(Oktaviana et al., 2025; Zhang \& Yin, 2021).

\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{2.3.1 Local-First Software
Architecture Paradigm}
}{2.3.1 Local-First Software Architecture Paradigm }}\label{local-first-software-architecture-paradigm}

Grounding the proposed system's technical design, the Local-First
Software Paradigm (Kleppmann et al., 2019) posits that data-centric
applications should execute core storage and processing functions
directly on client devices without requiring constant cloud
connectivity. In primary care environments characterized by variable
network infrastructure, local-first computing guarantees high
operational availability by enabling offline record management, local
querying, and offline analytics execution directly on edge hardware
(Kleppmann et al., 2019). Empirical evaluations of electronic health
record (EHR) deployments in Philippine primary care settings demonstrate
that system acceptability degrades when software architectures introduce
rigid connectivity or platform constraints across diverse health
facilities (Elepaño et al., 2025). By embedding a local-first SQLite
database paired with asynchronous cloud database synchronization, the
system provides local data ownership and operational continuity for
primary health units during network disruptions while maintaining
central data consolidation capability.

\textbf{2.3.2 Relational Database Theory}

Edgar F. Codd's (1970) foundational relational model provides the
mathematical structure for the proposed system's data management layer.
Relational database theory asserts that data integrity is maintained
through formal normalization rules, primary and foreign key constraints,
and typed attribute fields, which collectively eliminate data redundancy
and entry anomalies.

In healthcare and medical informatics, applying relational schemas
addresses the severe limitations of non-conventional flat-file or
spreadsheet-based tracking, which are prone to record corruption and
duplicate entries (Dilling, 2020; Thomer \& Wickett, 2020). By
structuring epidemiological variables such as Morbidity Week, Morbidity
Month, Barangay, Age, Sex, and Case Classification within a normalized
SQLite schema, the system enforces mathematical integrity constraints at
the point of entry. This guarantees that micro-level case data remain
structurally consistent and fully queryable for longitudinal analysis
(Dilling, 2020).

\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{2.3.3 Epidemiological Threshold and
Predictive Modeling
Principles}}{2.3.3 Epidemiological Threshold and Predictive Modeling Principles}}\label{epidemiological-threshold-and-predictive-modeling-principles}

Grounding the system\textquotesingle s analytical engine,
epidemiological surveillance theory establishes that public health
intervention should be driven by objective historical statistical
deviations rather than reactive observation (Ouédraogo et al., 2023). In
municipal health monitoring, raw case logs often suffer from temporal
gaps or reporting inconsistencies that can distort standard forecasting
methods (Al Mobin \& Kamrujjaman, 2023). To overcome these constraints,
surveillance frameworks utilize non-parametric historical baselines,
specifically the 75th percentile (P75) metric, to reliably identify
localized transmission anomalies while mitigating distortion from
extreme seasonal outliers (Rysava et al., 2026). Furthermore,
computational time-series forecasting principles, operationalized via
tree-based ensemble learning such as the Random Forest Regressor,
provide the theoretical basis for capturing non-linear temporal trends
in disease incidence (Zhao et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2025). This
theoretical foundation justifies the platform\textquotesingle s
multi-component architecture, ensuring that both retrospective baseline
comparisons and prospective case projections are mathematically sound
and operationally actionable for municipal health monitoring.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{2.3.4 Privacy Calculus Theory}
}{2.3.4 Privacy Calculus Theory }}\label{privacy-calculus-theory}

This theory provides the psychological and decision-making framework for
data sharing in health tracking systems. As established by Li et al.
(2023), individuals engage in a "calculus" where they weigh the
perceived privacy risks (e.g., data breaches or hardware theft) against
the perceived benefits of public service utility.

This theory justifies the inclusion of robust security measures such as
At-Rest Encryption and Credential Hashing not just as technical
requirements, but as essential tools to mitigate user risk perception.
By reducing the "potential loss expectations" through visible security
protocols, the system increases the user's intention to provide the
granular data necessary for accurate health monitoring.

\textbf{3.1 Conceptual Framework}

The conceptual architecture of this investigation utilizes an
Input-Process-Output (IPO) paradigm to operationalize the conversion of
raw community case logs into structured epidemiological surveillance. As
illustrated in Figure 3.1, the framework integrates flexible data
inputs, a dedicated backend processing and modeling pipeline, a secure
relational storage layer, and a web-based interface to facilitate
high-resolution tracking across municipal administrative
boundaries.\includegraphics[width=7.26173in,height=3.34449in]{./media/image6.png}

\textbf{Figure 3.1 \emph{Conceptual Framework of the System}}

\textbf{3.1.1 Input Stage}

The system initiation phase centers on capturing and ingesting granular
community health data through two main entry points:

Case Records Input: Accommodates flexible data acquisition via Manual
Point-of-Care Logging for individual case entry and Batch Dataset
Uploads (.xlsx / .csv) for bulk ingestion.

Data Ingestion Module: Executes the conversion and normalization of raw
community records into a standardized digital format suitable for
backend parsing and database integration.

\textbf{3.1.2 Process Stage}

The core processing infrastructure is structured across a backend
analytical pipeline and a secure database management layer:

Backend Processing \& Modeling Pipeline: Powered by a Python-based
processing engine that executes Temporal Trend Analysis \& P75 Threshold
Detection (evaluating weekly/monthly trends against historical
baselines) and runs a Random Forest Predictive Model for forward case
projection and risk classification.

SQLite Database Storage Layer: Serves as the persistence backbone,
utilizing a Structured Relational Database (SQLite \& SQLCipher) to
maintain Persistent Case Records Storage while ensuring strict data
security through Encrypted Storage at Rest.

\textbf{3.1.3 Output Stage}

The framework concludes by delivering processed intelligence and
interactive tools through the application interface:

Web-Based Management System (Flask): Acts as the core routing and
interface foundation, linking backend data to the user-facing
application via an interactive web interface.

Epidemiological Surveillance \& Alerting: The final output module
presents Automated Warning Alerts (Spike/Outbreak Detection) through
dynamic risk status banners (Green, Yellow, Red), Barangay Risk Ranking
(Case-Based Severity) via priority watchlists, and On-Demand Yearly
Reports \& Data Exports utilizing temporal year-filtering capabilities.

\textbf{3.2 Operational Framework}

The operational framework (see Figure 3.2) delineates the sequential
workflow and procedural stages required to execute the study. While the
conceptual framework defines the system's underlying architecture, the
operational framework serves as a procedural roadmap, mapping the
step-by-step transition from initial health record log ingestion through
secure local database storage, Python-based analytical processing, Flask
web routing, and final dashboard rendering.

\includegraphics[width=7.3951in,height=1.13603in]{./media/image5.png}

\textbf{Figure 3.2 Operational Framework Of The System}

\textbf{3.2.1 Phase I: Data Acquisition and Ingestion}

The initial phase focuses on capturing and formatting raw community
health data:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Flexible Input Methods: Accommodates data capture through manual
  point-of-care entry for individual cases as well as batch dataset
  uploads (.xlsx / .csv) for bulk historical records.
\item
  Schema Normalization: The ingestion pipeline handles minor header
  formatting variations across datasets, normalizing fields such as
  Morbidity Week, clinical classifications, and demographic attributes
  into a unified relational schema.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{3.2.2 Phase II: Secure Local Storage and Database Management}

This procedural stage governs data persistence, structural integrity,
and privacy compliance within the local environment:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  SQLite \& SQLCipher Storage: Normalized records are written to a
  structured local relational database secured with SQLCipher, ensuring
  robust encryption at rest for sensitive health information.
\item
  Data Privacy Compliance: To comply with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data
  Privacy Act of 2012), direct personal identifiers are stripped or
  anonymized, maintaining secure internal record tracking.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{3.2.3 Phase III: Python Processing and Modeling Pipeline}

The analytical core executes automated computations upon data retrieval:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Temporal Trend \& P75 Threshold Detection: Evaluates weekly and
  monthly case trends against historical baselines, applying a
  non-parametric 75th-percentile (P75) statistical threshold to identify
  localized epidemic triggers.
\item
  Random Forest Predictive Model: Runs classification and projection
  algorithms to forecast short-term case trajectories and assess
  outbreak risk tiers.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{3.2.4 Phase IV: Flask Web Application Routing}

This intermediary layer connects the analytical backend to the user
interface:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Web Application Routing: Utilizes the Flask framework to handle
  server-side routing, user authentication management, and dynamic
  session handling, ensuring secure access for authorized health
  personnel.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{3.2.5 Phase V: Surveillance Dashboard and Data Exports}

The final operational stage delivers actionable intelligence to
municipal end-users:

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Interactive Surveillance Dashboard: Renders automated warning alert
  banners (Green, Yellow, Red states based on threshold breaches),
  barangay risk rankings via priority watchlists, and multi-year
  temporal filtering.
\item
  On-Demand Reporting: Compiles processed data and analytics into
  structured views and export formats to support municipal public health
  decision-making and vector control interventions.
\end{itemize}
